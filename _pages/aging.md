# Research in Aging, Language and Cognition  

Early detection of cognitive decline, particularly mild cognitive impairment (MCI), has been of increasing interest for enriching clinical trials and enhancing the effectiveness of various classes of drugs that seek to change disease progression through early intervention. A focus on behavioral markers (such as language production and comprehension) of cognitive decline offers significant cost effectiveness and accessibility compared to biological markers using PET or CT scans. Current diagnostic tools for language disorders in aging are primarily focused on lexical aspects of language performance or structured conversations using fixed prompts, resulting in late diagnosis of dementia and limiting the nuanced evaluation of the relationship between language and cognitive control in predicting dementia. Furthermore, using language as a biomarker for dementia has been almost exclusively studied in monolingual populations, which potentially limits their usefulness in predicting dementia in linguistically diverse populations. The proposed research seeks to address these failures by providing novel diagnostic tools which focus on the interplay of cognitive control with sentence- and discourse-level language competency, and the applicability of these tools to linguistically diverse populations.  


This research is conducted by a subgroup of the Language, Cognition, and Communication Working Group, affiliated with the [Aging Research Network (ARN)](https://research.iu.edu/research-networks/aging/index.html) at Indiana University. Faculty on IUB 2030 working groups identified the areas — aging, environmental health, artificial intelligence and quantum technology — which were detailed in the strategic plan as high-priority fields in which to pursue large-scale external grant funding targeted at solving many of today’s biggest challenges. We are privileged to receive funding from IU to contribute to these goals.​
